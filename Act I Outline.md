# Act I Outline: The Gift

Companion to Story_Structure.md (Section 12A), Excerpt_Bank.md, and the Elastic Pace System (Revised). Act I covers Chapters A to C, 2050 to 2075, in 105 micro-chapters as currently cast. A1 to A3 are published. A4 is cast at 15 micro-chapters, released as three batches of 5 (author ruling). B1 to B4 are cast at 10 each, released as two batches of 5. C1 to C7 keep their original casts of 5. C1 to C5 and C7 are extended to 10 each when drafted, and C6 stays at 5 to offset A4's extension (decision). Movement names, endings, batch lines, echoes, wants, and re-cuts are proposals (decision).

## 1. Shape of the act

- **What changes.** Humanity is handed cures and the knowledge to deliver them, then ships and machines, by no one it can name. The reader sees who is behind the gift (Kenji), the network he builds so that no one can find him, the pressure that taught him to build it, and what it costs him. The cavern then shows that humanity was not first, and Charon turns out to be a door.
- **Ends on.** The road runs out at the second jump, and in the letters dread overtakes fascination.
- **The engine.** Each chapter answers one question and raises the next: who is giving us this and what does it cost them (A, B), what lies behind the dead, and will it return (C).
- **Tone.** Wonder in A, strain in B, unease in C. The act closes on a line the in-universe record supplies: after the relay, the question "belonged to locksmiths."
- **Link to the Prologue.** A1, A2, and A3 are the Prologue's three doors (the body, the stars, the work of the world). A4 is the question the Prologue says no one could answer, and it now answers it from inside.
- **Dramatic irony.** From A4 the reader sees what the in-universe history cannot. Each excerpt is the scholar's account, and the micro-chapters complete or contradict it.

## 2. Chapter by chapter

Batch line: what a batch of five ends on emotionally. Echo: the earlier element a batch returns to. Wants: the small want and deadline given to each exposition narrator.

### Movement 2: The Gamble

**A4. The Inventor Nobody Could Name** (2050 to 2068)

A4 runs at 15 micro-chapters and Kenji carries 6 of them (author ruling, replacing the cap of 3 or 4 for this chapter). It jumps back to 2050 after A3 ends in 2069, a flashback block whose seam falls between batches. Three rules govern A4a and A4b:
- **The conspiracy starts with the release of his tech (author ruling).** The first private release in 2051 draws the first probe. Every pre-2065 incident (A4.3, A4.6, A4.8) is read by every POV as ordinary greed. The reader sees the same shape in each (a cutout, a clean paper trail, no name behind it) and no character does.
- **The secret network exists before the Shepards (author ruling).** Shells, managers, clinics, and the hidden service are built in A4.2 to A4.5, before Galveston opens in A4.7. The Shepards walk into an organization that is already running. Brooks (A4.10) is a hire into a service that already exists.
- **The operatives arrive with the network (author ruling).** The first cadre operatives are recruited in A4.4, as part of building the network. They do the service's work from then on: they answer the probes, watch the troublemakers, and later scout and pitch Brooks. They carry no POV, so servants stay in the background. The reader meets them only through Kenji, the thief, Tom, and Ruth, and they are never named in a byline.
- **No Reaper thoughts and no past-life reading thoughts before 2065.** Kenji's knowledge arrives in the hands before the head.

## 5. Constraints

- **Kenji's POV.** From A4 his POV carries his thoughts. Before 2065 he has no Reaper thoughts and no thought that this was once a fiction he read, and the knowledge arrives in his hands before his head. From 2065 the reader sees the Reapers. He carries 6 of A4's 15 micro-chapters and at most 3 or 4 in every other Movement 2 chapter. His own chapters never name shaking hands, and the body carries the feeling through squaring objects and washing his hands. No character is told how he knows what he knows.
- **The conspiracy (author ruling).** It begins with the release of his tech, so the early pressure is its first moves. No POV before 2065 links the incidents, and none names it. Brooks logs the earlier incidents as unrelated files. No in-universe document connects them. The four delegates of 2125 are partly responsible for the 2059 bomb and the 2065 withdrawals only. The earlier probes stay unattributed, so the conspiracy remains larger than those four.
- **The network (author ruling).** The secret network exists before Kenji meets the Shepards, and its operatives are recruited with it in A4.4, before Galveston opens. Operatives carry no POV (author ruling). They scout Brooks and make her recruitment pitch. This revises the earlier 2058 to 2062 date for the service.
- **Documents.** No in-universe document names the conspiracy, the true cause of the 2065 withdrawals, or Kenji's role in the flare. His name may appear in documents for minor matters.
- **Reapers.** No one outside the inner circle knows or suspects the Reapers. Chapters about "what is on the other side" read as a reaction to the dead, and Kenji's only stated justification to others is the hypothetical about whoever killed the dead.
- **Hannah's dissent** is the only dissent seen in this act. Hannah, Brooks, and Manswell see him from outside.
- No human meets an alien, and the Citadel appears nowhere.
- **Dates** open in the bible (the flare, the reveal, the first public crossing) are proposals. The A4 places and the exact dates of A4.1 to A4.6 are proposals.
- **Register (author ruling).** What the excerpts say about how the written accounts read applies to the in-universe record only. The chapters carry full emotion.
- **Style (author ruling).** The Elastic Pace System, revised for serialization, governs every chapter. In the Fast Gear, named thoughts and feelings are stripped but one body detail stays.
