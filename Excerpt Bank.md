# Prologue Excerpt Bank: Major Events

**Labels (author ruling).** Parts are the seven groups, Part A to Part G. Chapters are the units A1, A2, A3, and so on (32 in all). Micro-chapters are the single-POV pieces inside a chapter, coded A1.1, A1.2, A2.1, and so on (193 in all). Each chapter opens with its excerpt, the quoted passage from the in-universe history that serves as its epigraph and base.

Excerpts for use as the base of the main-story chapters (author ruling, Story Structure Section 12), drawn from the personal account and kept to the major events of the history from 2050 to 2175. The author's presence has been removed: the excerpts carry no first-person voice, no personal asides, and no sign of who wrote them. Passages have been trimmed and lightly reworded where that was needed, so they read as an impartial in-universe account. Every excerpt holds only information the Protagonist has sanctioned.


## POV Casting

Each chapter carries a cast of single-POV micro-chapters, placed directly under its excerpt. Every chapter has the five-micro-chapter minimum from Story Structure Section 12. Parts D to F carry 33 added micro-chapters (marked "added" in each cast heading) from the D to F expansion, which also introduces ten recurring original characters (see Recurring OCs below). Most POVs are characters from Human_Characters.md. The rest are marked OC and are limited to light exposition or general outlook, or are one of the ten named recurring OCs. There are 193 micro-chapters over 32 chapters, at about 142,850 words (investigative about 800, action about 550, hybrid about 750). The 33 added micro-chapters come to about 22,700 words, under the expansion's 25,000-word cap, with about 2,300 words spare.

**Casting rules.**
- A file character carries POV only from age 8, only while alive, and only for what that character could see or know. Ages in each cast line are computed from the story birth year and checked against death years.
- The Protagonist's choice of empire over democracy meets resistance from Hannah and Brooks (D1.4, E1.1, F2.1, F2.2). His justification is that democracy has had its chance and its endurance has been severely lacking beside Rome's, whose legacies are far better.
- The Protagonist carries no POV, because his POV would expose that he is a transmigrator (Section 3). Before 2125 he appears only as seen by Hannah, Brooks, and Manswell. The colonial league's master who declares and proclaims in D4 is him, so from then on he is the public Emperor, seen by every POV.
- Before 2072 the only file characters old enough to carry POV are Hannah Shepard, Maya Brooks, and Victor Manswell. Parts A to C therefore lean on those three, and up to two of the five micro-chapters per chapter are OC slots. In Parts D to G one original slot per chapter is OC at most. For the D to F additions, rule 19 is relaxed: at most three of the five new slots may be OC, and Part G keeps one.
- The Reaper-war generation (Alexandra, Kaidan, Joker, Ashley, Vega, Jacob, Archer, Jenkins) appears only in the 2160s and 2170s. The secret of the Reapers is known only to Hannah (from 2170), Brooks, and Alexandra (from her twenty-first birthday). No one outside the inner circle knows or suspects.
- The lender squeeze and the true nature of the investing firm stay out of every in-universe document. The killing of the Union delegates appears in no chapter or document except as the act of a rival superpower, the Russian Federation. No writing contains concrete evidence of Inquisition or hidden-service involvement.
- No human meets an alien in these chapters, and the Citadel appears nowhere.
- Dates the bible leaves open (Charon activation, cavern reveal method, relay removal) are marked or chosen as proposals.

**Rules for the recurring OCs.**

OC (outlook) is a general-outlook voice. OC (exposition) carries light exposition. OC (action) is an action voice.

## Part A: Gifts Without a Giver
*The cures and the medical knowledge behind them, the first ships, the new machines, and the unanswered question of where they came from.*

### A1. Those Who Walked Out Whole

The clinics were built around a single apparatus, the Med-Pod. Patients entered it ill or broken and, by their own accounts, left restored. The accounts do not gush. They are written in a stunned, flat register, like those of survivors of an accident who have not yet decided whether to believe they are alive. Patients crossed borders to reach the clinics. Families waited to see a cure work on a neighbor before trusting it with their own. By the 2060s the clinics stood in all the larger economies, though standing everywhere is a different matter from being accepted.


**POV cast (5 micro-chapters)**

- **A1.1 · Hannah Shepard (22), 2053.** Galveston clinic. Her father, dying of pancreatic cancer, walks out of the Med-Pod and asks for breakfast. She is offered the upload and declines it, and her father does not press her. *[I]* Hook: A card with no name on it is slid under the clinic door.
- **A1.2 · OC (exposition), 2058.** A mother carries a dying child across a border to a clinic and joins a queue of families waiting to watch a neighbor's cure work before trusting it with their own. *[I]* Hook: The neighbor's boy runs past her, and the clerk calls a number that is not hers.
- **A1.3 · OC (outlook), 2059.** A district official sent to close a clinic stands in its waiting room among the people it has cured. He watches a man walk who was carried in the week before, and goes home. *[I]* Hook: His report to the ministry says only: 'Nothing found.'
- **A1.4 · Hannah Shepard (28), 2059.** Five months pregnant at the Galveston clinic complex when a bomb goes off in the car park. She gets the night staff out. She survives, but the child she carries does not. *[A]* Hook: In the corridor her husband says there will be no child until no one can reach her, and she agrees.
- **A1.5 · Maya Brooks (24), 2062.** Dying of leukemia in a Med-Pod ward, she is found by a recruiter and walks out cured, still counting exits from habit. *[I]* Hook: The recruiter asks one question: what would she do with sixty more years?

### A2. The Centuries That Became Days

Then, in the 2060s, ships appeared that could cross to another star in days. The papers of the time give their pace as about a light year a day. Alpha Centauri lies a little over four light years from Sol, so the trip took four days or so. Until that decade no human being had gone beyond the Solar System, and Alpha Centauri had been, for every earlier generation, a destination fit only for the dreams of grandchildren's grandchildren. The chronicles show crowds at the first departures, and governments scrambling to understand what had been placed in their hands.


**POV cast (5 micro-chapters)**

- **A2.1 · Maya Brooks (25), 2063.** Collection. She tallies three ministries scrambling to buy the design and finds each is asking the same question. *[I]* Hook: Every form answers it with the same blank line: who built it.
- **A2.2 · Hannah Shepard (33), 2064.** Escort lead on the first long-range test ship. Four days to Alpha Centauri at a light year a day, and the first people ever to pass beyond the Solar System. *[I]* Hook: The drive stutters at the midpoint, and no one aboard can say why it should.
- **A2.3 · OC (outlook), 2065.** An old man in the dockside crowd, who dreamed of stars as a boy, watches the first scheduled departure and weeps without knowing why. *[I]* Hook: The berth is empty and the crowd is still cheering.
- **A2.4 · OC (exposition), 2065.** A procurement officer is told to understand what has been placed in his government's hands. His engineers cannot find a single patent. *[I]* Hook: The one lead he has is a firm that has existed for eleven months.
- **A2.5 · Hannah Shepard (35), 2066.** An escort ship loses its drive mid-crossing. She brings it home inside the flotilla's field and runs the drill she wrote for exactly this. *[A]* Hook: The field collapse she is watching is not the one in the drill.

### A3. Cheap Hands and Angry Ones

Within the decade came power that did not depend on fuel markets, machines that could raise a district from raw material in a season, and robots able to do almost any manual task. Each of these threw someone out of work or into debt. The builders met the new machines first with disbelief and then with orders. The labor disputes of the late 2060s are among the best documented events of the age. The strikers were furious about losing their jobs, exactly as people always have been, and the fact that the thing taking their jobs was a robot from nowhere hardly seemed to trouble them.


**POV cast (5 micro-chapters)**

- **A3.1 · OC (outlook), 2067.** A shipyard welder, laid off the week a robot crew raised a district in a season, marches with the strikers. He is furious about the job, not the robot. *[I]* Hook: Someone three rows back in the march is carrying a gun.
- **A3.2 · Hannah Shepard (36), 2067.** Her security firm guards an automation site. The picket becomes a surge, and she holds the gate. *[A]* Hook: The first stone comes from the roof behind the strikers, not from them.
- **A3.3 · Maya Brooks (30), 2068.** Influence. She is told to fund the strikers' legal defense and the politicians who will write the retraining acts. The note is unsigned. *[I]* Hook: It is in a hand she knows, and it says: let them be angry, keep them alive.
- **A3.4 · OC (exposition), 2068.** A construction foreman meets the new machines with disbelief, then with orders. He is asked to train the crew that replaces his own. *[I]* Hook: The machine finishes his drawing before he does.
- **A3.5 · Hannah Shepard (38), 2069.** She hires laid-off men into her security firm and finds the strikers' anger has a use. *[I]* Hook: One of them asks who owns the robots, and she gives the honest answer: she does not know all of it.

### A4. The Inventor Nobody Could Name

Where did all of this come from? Every history of invention has false starts, rival claimants, patent quarrels, and a famous laboratory or two. This one has none. The firms that sold the ships, the machines, and the clinics were young, and when asked how they had come by their designs they gave vague answers, every time, without exception. People at the time guessed at a recovered foreign program, a private genius, a theft from some government. No named inventor, university, or state laboratory has ever been found, and no evidence supports any of the guesses.


**POV cast (5 micro-chapters)**

- **A4.1 · Maya Brooks (26), 2064.** She drafts the answer every firm will give when asked about its designs: vague, consistent, and never the same words twice. *[I]* Hook: The first press query arrives before the ink is dry.
- **A4.2 · OC (outlook), 2066.** An investigative journalist chases the inventor through patent offices, university archives and state laboratories and finds nothing in any of them. *[I]* Hook: The one clerk who remembers the filing has been on leave for a year.
- **A4.3 · Hannah Shepard (35), 2066.** At a dinner she is asked who builds the ships. She gives the answer she was given and watches the questioner decide she is lying. *[I]* Hook: His card names a ministry that does not appear on any chart.
- **A4.4 · OC (exposition), 2068.** A state analyst follows a money trail through eleven jurisdictions and meets layered holding companies with a servant-manager at the top of each branch. *[H]* Hook: The last shell is a laundromat with a very good lawyer.
- **A4.5 · Maya Brooks (30), 2068.** Action layer, last resort. A rival probe gets too close. She ends it with paper only, a forged trail and a transfer, and no one is harmed. *[A]* Hook: The probe's lead thanks her for the tidy file. He has noticed the tidiness.

## Part B: A Gamble on the Nearest Star
*The expedition, the withdrawn lenders, the rescue, and the spark.*

### B1. More Than a Voyage

Victor Manswell announced an expedition to Alpha Centauri in 2065. The trip itself took days. The hard part was what he meant to do on arrival, which was to leave people there to live. A colony needs homes built before anyone sleeps in them, supplies to last until it can feed itself, and armed ships to stand guard while it does. All of that had to be made, paid for, and carried across before the first settler could be left behind.


**POV cast (5 micro-chapters)**

- **B1.1 · Victor Manswell (50), 2065.** Announces the Alpha Centauri expedition to a hall of lenders. He does not say the hard part aloud: leaving people there to live. *[I]* Hook: A lender asks how many ships will stay to guard them, and he has no answer yet.
- **B1.2 · Hannah Shepard (34), 2065.** Escort flotilla commander. She reads the manifest and finds the guard ships are what the lenders will cut first. That night her husband goes silent, then asks her to move work farther from Sol. *[I]* Hook: He says 'farther' twice and nothing else, and she does not ask.
- **B1.3 · OC (outlook), 2065.** A would-be settler reads the announcement over a kitchen table and weighs a mortgage against a star. *[I]* Hook: She sets the mortgage papers aside and writes her name on the interest list.
- **B1.4 · Victor Manswell (50), 2065.** His ledger: homes built before anyone sleeps in them, stores to last until a colony feeds itself, armed ships to stand guard. The sum is more than the company is worth. *[I]* Hook: The first lender's letter is already in the stack.
- **B1.5 · Maya Brooks (27), 2065.** Collection. The cadre's reports of familiar organizations resolve into a name, and the Protagonist asks for the file at three in the morning. *[I]* Hook: His hands are not steady when he takes it.

### B2. The Money That Fled All at Once

At the start he had backers. Lenders in several economies subscribed after the announcement. Then, within a few months, they pulled out one after another, withdrawing their money or demanding repayment in a rush. The newspapers offered two explanations, doubts about the safety of his ships and a sudden squeeze on credit, and the sources do not choose between them. People at the time noticed how closely the withdrawals clustered, and no one proved a cause.


**POV cast (5 micro-chapters)**

- **B2.1 · Victor Manswell (50), 2065.** Lenders in several economies call. Each says it is coincidence. He counts the calls until he can stop. *[I]* Hook: A rival's chief calls to offer condolences before the deal is dead.
- **B2.2 · OC (outlook), 2065.** A bank risk officer pulls his house out of the expedition and tells himself it is safety doubts. The credit committee decided before he spoke. *[I]* Hook: His signature is on a memo he does not remember writing.
- **B2.3 · Maya Brooks (27), 2065.** She compiles the timeline of withdrawals, with dates, amounts and counterparties, and leaves the cause field blank by rule. *[I]* Hook: The clustering is too tidy even for her.
- **B2.4 · Hannah Shepard (34), 2065.** She waits at Sol with the escort flotilla as the guard contracts are cancelled one by one, and is told to hold the crews on retainer for a different client. *[I]* Hook: The new client's name is not on the retainer.
- **B2.5 · Victor Manswell (50), 2065.** Board vote with an empty treasury. The safety review is leaked, and he reads both explanations in the same morning edition. *[H]* Hook: A courier arrives from an investing firm no one has heard of.

### B3. A Bargain Struck at the Brink

Stripped of funding, Manswell was left with too few ships, built too lightly, and no escort. As the venture neared collapse he took money and better ship designs from an investing firm, and gave up control of his own company in exchange. He stayed on as its public face. What the firm was, and who stood behind it, the sources do not say.


**POV cast (5 micro-chapters)**

- **B3.1 · Victor Manswell (50), 2065.** He meets the investing firm's representative in a glass room. The terms are money, safer ship designs, a longevity treatment, and control of his company in return for staying on as its public face. *[I]* Hook: He signs, then asks to read what he signed.
- **B3.2 · Hannah Shepard (34), 2065.** She reads the new ship specifications and finds the hulls are what she asked for months ago and was told did not exist. *[I]* Hook: The bill of materials names a yard she has never heard of.
- **B3.3 · Maya Brooks (27), 2065.** She prepares Manswell's manager-grade file, what he is told and what he is not, and wonders what it is to sign a company away and still smile in public. *[I]* Hook: His first words on receiving it are 'Who are you people?' and she is not allowed to answer.
- **B3.4 · OC (outlook), 2065.** Manswell's chief engineer, who thought the ships too light, watches the new designs arrive and quietly stops complaining. *[I]* Hook: He finds his own earlier objection already answered in the margin.
- **B3.5 · Victor Manswell (50), 2065.** The night after. He stands on the yard roof with the first rescued hull behind him, knowing he no longer owns it. *[H]* Hook: A clinician arrives to take his first blood.

### B4. The Day the Stars Became a Plan

The rescue worked. In the late 2060s the expedition sailed and founded a permanent settlement at Alpha Centauri. The world watched the departure, and the effect was immediate. Subscriptions, charter applications, and requests for passage multiplied within months, and governments and corporations that had treated the stars as a speculation began to plan for them seriously. The colonizing wave of the 2070s grew directly out of what his company had proved possible.

## Part D: A Thousand Flags

### D1. As Far as the Ships Could Safely Go

## Part F: The Reckoning
*The reckoning after the war, the proclamation recalled, the relay quarantine, and the closed relays.*

### F1. One Law, One Reckoning

The terms had been stated on the first day of the war and never changed: a single body of law under the Imperium of Sol, and for every polity a choice between conceding and facing justice, or being crushed.
