## 9B. Legion Backgrounds and Characteristics

Created at the author's instruction. Names, colors, and characters for the 4th to 10th Legions are mine, and the author may revise them.

### How the Legions are filled (author ruling, with inference)

- **Veterans lead.** A quarter of every Legion are veterans, and they fill every authoritative position. The other three quarters are aspirants and acolytes recruited from the Legion's home and chapter worlds. There are no artificial humans. All ten Legions are complete by 2175.
- (inference) Aspirants are applicants from the home worlds. Acolytes are the ones chosen for training. A candidate becomes a Legionnaire when he receives the Primaris Process and the conditioning layer. (open) The ages and length of training.
- (inference) A brother raised from the home worlds carries his own life with him: a family, a world, and a local tradition. Each Legion's traditions, grudges, and humor still come from its veteran leaders, and the recruits take them up beside their own.
- (inference) Every Legionnaire, veteran or recruited, receives the Primaris Process and the conditioning layer, wears Astartes-type armor, and uses the same address, Brother plus rank (see Section 10). Nothing in rank or address marks a brother's origin.
- (inference) If the nine later Legions began with only their veterans, the home worlds supply at most about 675,000 recruits from 2160 to 2175. That is about 45,000 a year, or about 123 a day. Across 99 home and chapter worlds it is roughly 455 per world per year, which is easily sustainable.
- (inference) Each Legion has two colors on its armor.

### The Legions

**1st Legion, The Founders.** Raised 2106. Iron grey and bone white. Station: Adamant. Homeworld: Ironweld.
- Background. Raised from the mercenary companies that their employers abandoned in the proxy skirmishes of 2090 to 2105. They are the oldest veterans in the Imperium.
- Character. Patient, methodical, and slow to speak. They build defenses in depth and rehearse them until the drill is dull. Once a year the Legion reads aloud the names of the companies their paymasters left behind, and the brothers raised on Ironweld learn those names as their own history.

**2nd Legion, The Gunwales.** Raised 2119. Deep blue and brass. Station: mobile. Homeworld: Calder Maxim.
- Background. Raised from boarding crews, ship's marines, and damage-control petty officers of the naval clashes of 2105 to 2120, drawn from every bloc that fought them.
- Character. Close-quarters fighters who judge a plan by how it works in a corridor two meters wide. Each captain paints a keel stripe on his armor for every hull he has boarded, and a recruited brother earns his first stripe on his first boarding. Their humor centers on air supply and bulkheads.

**3rd Legion, The Wayfarers.** Raised 2123. Sand and dark green. Station: mobile. Homeworld: Sable Verin.
- Background. Raised from convoy escorts, blockade-runners, and raiders of the blockades and supply-line raids.
- Character. Mobile and frugal. Leaders keep personal route logs and teach the recruits to read an enemy's supply before his guns. They ran the raids themselves, so they know how raiders think and hunt them well.

**4th Legion, The Stonebreakers.** Raised 2120. Slate grey and rust red. Station: Austrine Bastion. Homeworld: Hollowgate.
- Background. Raised from siege engineers and assault infantry of the colony sieges of the 2090 to 2120 skirmishes, mostly in UNAS and Chinese People's Federation pay.
- Character. Stubborn and patient. A Stonebreaker officer plans a breach in days and states the number before the first shot. They describe problems as stone, with hard seams and soft walls.

**5th Legion, The Ashwalkers.** Raised 2121. Ash grey and soot black. Station: mobile. Homeworld: Pyre of Callis.
- Background. Raised from the brigade veterans of the town-by-town fighting of the 2090 to 2120 skirmishes, in the young colonies and relay settlements.
- Character. Silent and grim. They treat a district as a fortress made of rooms, and their leaders keep maps of cities that no longer exist. Their armor carries a scorched finish.

**6th Legion, The Skyfallen.** Raised 2122. Sky blue and white. Station: mobile. Homeworld: Halcyon Ferris.
- Background. Raised from airborne troops, orbital-drop jumpers, and special-forces veterans.
- Character. Confident and well rehearsed. Each jumper keeps a token from his first drop, and a recruited brother receives his token after his own. They rehearse every drop until the landing is routine.

**7th Legion, The Sappers.** Raised 2122. Ochre and black. Station: Fortuna. Homeworld: Auric Thule.
- Background. Raised from combat engineers, mine-clearers, demolition teams, and recovery crews.
- Character. Dry humor about fuses and tolerances. Every charge is laid from a written plan, and every plan is measured twice.

**8th Legion, The Concord.** Raised 2123. Green and white. Station: Han. Homeworld: Sanctus Orrin.
- Background. Raised from line officers and sergeants of the contract forces of many powers, men who served beside former enemies in the skirmishes.
- Character. Disciplined and adaptable. The Legion takes pride in being unremarkable and reliable, and no company bears the mark of a single origin.

**9th Legion, The Chartered.** Raised 2124. Navy blue and gold. Station: Ming. Homeworld: Eirene.
- Background. Raised from contract soldiers, corporate security officers, and mercenary captains.
- Character. Precise reports, legal phrasing, and ledgers of expenditure and loss. They regard the Oath as the one contract that never closes.

**10th Legion, The Remnant.** Raised 2124. Black with a scrap of each lost unit's color. Station: Arcturus (reserve). Homeworld: Eunomia.
- Background. Raised from survivors of units destroyed in the skirmishes and naval clashes of 2090 to 2120, often the last living soldier of a company.
- Character. Mournful and stubborn. Each company carries the banner of a dead unit, and the recruited brothers inherit it as their own. They do not leave a position they were ordered to hold.

## 10. Legionnaires: Armor, Background, and Speech

### Armor

- Legionnaires wear **Astartes-type** power armor made of Gundarium Gamma, with exactly the same form as the 40K versions. A Legionnaire's Primaris level is half that of a full Primaris Astartes, and an officer's or NCO's is three quarters (see Section 10D). Every Legion rank has the increased height and build.

### Background

- The leadership of every Legion (sergeant and above) are old mercenaries and veterans, drawn from every period of the conflicts, who received new leases on life in return for loyalty and service to the Protagonist. A quarter of every Legion are veterans in all. The other three quarters are aspirants and acolytes recruited from the Legion's home and chapter worlds, and there are no artificial humans (see Section 9B and Section 9C).
- The veterans were recruited from the impoverished, the crippled, and the dying.
- Only the Protagonist is addressed as "Lord."

### Speech and Address (follow 40K lore)

- Legionnaires are formal, terse, and economical. They use few or no contractions.
- They address each other by rank, with "Brother" in front, as in Brother-Sergeant and Brother-Captain.
- They address superiors by rank and never say "sir."
- They refer to one another collectively as brothers or battle-brothers.
- Dry understatement and grim humor are allowed.
- Their reports are formal and practical, for example "Objective secured" and not "Objective clear."
- **Weapon jams:** Legionnaires never panic. They rack the charging handle with brute force, rip out the jammed shell, or smash the enemy with the gun's heavy armored body until it works again.
- (inference) Aspirants and acolytes are not yet Legionnaires. They are not addressed as Brother, and they do not use the Legion's forms until they receive the Primaris Process.

## 10C. The Imperial Guard

- **Source.** Recruited from regular citizens of the Imperium and slightly enhanced, short of the Primaris Process.
- **Equipment.** Ordinary MIPS or CMC.
- **Transport.** The Guard has no ships of its own and relies on the Navy to carry it, so it moves at the Navy's drives: Mark II gravitic and Level Three warp.
- **Grade.** Rank and file are Staff: no secrets and no conditioning directive. Guard elites, officers, and NCOs are partially conditioned and receive Primaris at one quarter (1/4), with Tier 2 to 4 biotics (author ruling, see Section 10D). Their height and build stay human.
- **Role (author ruling).** The Guard garrisons all colonized worlds, including agri-worlds, livestock worlds, forge worlds, hive worlds, mining worlds, and resort worlds, and all stellar installations and facilities. After the Navy clears a system, the Guard holds its stations and docks. It also fills the gaps at the Adamant Systems while the Legions there are below full strength, alongside the Navy.
- **Relation to the Legions.** The Legions are the official army of the Imperium and its field force. The Guard holds the ground the Legions and the Navy have taken or secured.
- **Behavior.** Because they are unconditioned, Guard troops answer in individual voices. The rank and file are the main Imperial group an alien could realistically turn. Elites, officers, and NCOs are partially conditioned and harder targets. (inference) Aspirants, acolytes, and auxiliaries are also unconditioned, but they hold no secrets and are not on the front line.
- (inference) If each of the roughly 11,000 systems within 100 ly held a thousand Guard troops, the Guard would number about 11 million. Most systems hold only automated stations and outposts, so the real figure is open and probably lower. It is small against a population of 50 to 90 billion.

### The power gap against the Charter Front (decision)

- Inquisition members are conditioned, altered-Primaris, and Adjudicator-armored, at three quarters of a full Primaris, half again a rank-and-file Legionnaire. The Front's best technology is public-grade Med-Pod rebuilt from archives. The two are not comparable.
