# Story Bible: Mass Effect AU Novel

Only details the author has explicitly settled are listed here. Open questions are in Story_Structure.md, Section 13.

**Authority rule.** New information supersedes everything that has already been established. Anything affected by new information is obsolete and must be revised to align with it (see the Writing Rules below and the Revision Log).

**Status labels.** An entry the author states, or approves with "Auto approved," is fact. (inference) means a consequence drawn from settled facts. (open) means unsettled. (author ruling) means the author stated it in conversation and it supersedes earlier text.

**Process.** The story is written chapter by chapter, starting with the Prologue (see Story_Structure.md, Section 12).

## Writing Rules

**Reaper secrecy (decision).** Only the Protagonist and his inner circle (Hannah, Brooks, and Alexandra from her twenty-first birthday) know of the Reapers before they appear. No one else knows or suspects. The Imperium's forces train for every contingency their commanders can imagine, as any army does, and no one finds that remarkable. No chapter, document, or character outside the inner circle may refer to an unnamed enemy that the drills are for.

**Chapter excerpt header (author ruling).** Every chapter draft opens with its excerpt from Excerpt_Bank.md, because the excerpt is the base of the chapter. The excerpt sits at the very top of the draft, above any chapter title or byline, and is set as if quoted from a historical work:
1. The excerpt's title only, as a heading, with no code (A1) and no "Excerpt" label.
2. The excerpt text, word for word from the bank, in italics and enclosed in quotation marks.
3. An attribution line below it, a dash and the source in italics. The source is "The Road to the Stars", the Prologue's title, with no author named, so the unattributed voice holds (inference: the attribution is mine, and the author may change it).
4. Then the chapter's own title, byline, and micro-chapter prose.
Every micro-chapter draft of the excerpt carries the header.

**Excerpt register (author ruling).** What an excerpt says about how the written accounts read, such as the A1 excerpt's "stunned, flat register" and "the accounts do not gush", describes the in-universe record only. It does not apply to the chapters written from the excerpts and must not flatten them. Emotion is essential in the chapters. Characters feel, show, and are moved by what happens to them. The Elastic Pace gears still set how emotion is carried: inside the character's head in the Slow Gear, and through action and the body in the Fast Gear.

**Contrast conjunctions (author ruling).** Use "but", not "and", when the second clause cuts against what the first clause leads the reader to expect. An event whose expected result fails to follow, a sign that is not borne out, or a fact that undercuts the one before it takes "but". Use "and" only when the second clause adds to the first or follows from it. The test is the reader's expectation: if the first clause sets up a confirmation and the second clause refuses it, the word is "but". Example: "A card slides under the door, but the step is empty," not "and the step is empty." The rule matters most at a chapter's close, where a quiet reversal or an empty result carries the hook (inference: the test and the scope are mine, and the author may change them).
