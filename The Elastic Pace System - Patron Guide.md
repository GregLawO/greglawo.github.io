# How *The Road to the Stars* Is Written: The Elastic Pace System

## The idea

I write like an accordion. When the story is quiet, the prose expands to carry atmosphere, detail, and what a character is thinking. The moment danger arrives, it contracts into short, hard lines that move as fast as a pulse. Two storytelling philosophies share the page: the slow, methodical *investigation* and the fast, stripped-down *action*.

## The two gears

**The Slow Gear** is for arriving in a room, studying a clue, listening to a witness, or working out a problem.

- Paragraphs are moderately dense, never more than four sentences, so they read comfortably on a phone.
- Sentences are mostly flowing and descriptive, with an occasional short one for emphasis.
- The focus is on the setting (smell, weather, the weight of a place) and on what the character notices and concludes.
- Emotion lives in what the character notices. What a person notices tells you who they are.

**The Fast Gear** is for the instant something goes wrong.

- Paragraphs break into one or two lines.
- Sentences drop to short, blunt fragments.
- The camera moves to the body and the physical moment.
- Named thoughts and named feelings drop away, but the body keeps the feeling: a shaking hand, a held breath, a grip that will not open.

**How the switch works.** A sudden event (a sound, a betrayal, a deadline) cuts a paragraph off mid-thought. The sentences shrink, the thinking words vanish, and one physical detail stays behind to carry the emotion.

## The micro-chapter format

- **Micro-chapters.** Each micro-chapter is two to four pages. Investigative micro-chapters run roughly 600 to 1,000 words, action micro-chapters 400 to 700.
- **One point of view per micro-chapter.** The narrator changes every micro-chapter. You are only ever inside one head.
- **Roughly 80/20.** Most of a micro-chapter is Slow Gear. The last few paragraphs shift into Fast Gear.
- **An opening that asks a question.** Every micro-chapter begins on a concrete, specific detail, not a general scene-setting.

## How micro-chapters end

The Slow-to-Fast shift is the default close, but what it carries rotates, so five micro-chapters in a row never end the same way:

| Ending | What it feels like |
|---|---|
| **Physical event** | Something happens, and the lines get short |
| **Quiet reversal** | Nothing moves, but one fact changes everything |
| **Held breath** | A decision is about to be made, and the micro-chapter stops |
| **Withheld answer** | Someone is about to speak, and doesn't |
| **Emotional beat** | A moment that closes without a hook |

## The weekly batch

Each release is **one chapter of five micros**, meant to be read in a single sitting.

- A batch opens with an **epigraph**, a short passage quoted from a history of the age. It tells you what the batch is about, as that history would describe it.
- Each micro-chapter carries a byline: **Act, Movement, batch code, and narrator**, followed by a place and a year.
- A batch has a shape: one anchor micro-chapter, a quieter middle, and the strongest hook last.
- Each batch echoes something from the one before it. If an object or phrase returns, it is not an accident.

The epigraphs describe how the *recorded history* reads. The micro-chapters themselves are full of feeling.

## Voices

Recurring narrators each pay attention to different things, so the book does not sound like one person:

| Narrator | What they notice |
|---|---|
| Hannah | What is missing |
| Brooks | Numbers, counts, and ledgers |
| Ordinary people | What they can touch and afford: prices, queues, the weight of a child |
| Manswell | Money, and who owns what |

Lore micro-chapters are told by ordinary people who want something small and urgent, so the world arrives through what they are trying to do.

## A sample of the switch

*Slow Gear:*

"The air inside the vault was thick with the scent of stagnant groundwater, a testament to decades of abandonment. Marcus adjusted the heavy belt riding low on his hips and moved toward the control console, tracing the wiring with his flashlight. Someone had deliberately cut the manual override switches from the inside."

*The trigger:*

"A loud, metallic snap echoed from the corridor behind him."

*Fast Gear, with the body carrying the feeling:*

"Marcus froze.

The beam caught a shadow in the doorway.

Iron scraped. The door slammed.

Water hissed from the vents. It hit his boots. It hit his shins.

He lunged. Slipped. His shoulder cracked against the console.

His hands were shaking. He wedged the crowbar in anyway.

The water reached his knees."
